## In one sentence

Write the paper's central contribution in your own words. What did the authors discover, demonstrate, or make possible?

## The research question

What problem does this paper address? Explain why the question matters and what was missing from earlier work.

## Approach & key findings

Describe the main idea before the technical details. Then collect the results that matter most to your understanding.

1. **The approach.** What data, experiment, model, or theoretical argument did the authors use?
2. **The evidence.** Which result most directly supports their conclusion?
3. **The limits.** Under what assumptions or conditions might the conclusion change?

> A useful reading note makes the reasoning easier to revisit, not just the abstract shorter.

## My reflections

This is the space for your own thinking. Connect the paper to something you already know, identify an assumption you want to challenge, or describe an idea you could use in your research.

### A connection worth exploring

What other paper or concept does this remind you of? Explain the connection while it is still fresh.

## Open questions

- What did I not fully understand?
- Which experiment or analysis would I try next?
- What should I read to fill in the gaps?

## Next reading

Add related papers and links here as your reading develops.
